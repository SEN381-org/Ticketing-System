Milestone 1 deliberately applies knowledge developed in earlier modules. Students are not expected to relearn stakeholder

analysis, requirements, programming or basic design concepts. Instead, they must use that prior knowledge as evidence

within a controlled Software Engineering environment.

Prior learning applied

•  Systems Analysis & Design: stakeholder identification and analysis; functional and non-functional requirements; scope

and system boundaries; modelling; documentation; and analysis of competing needs.

•  Programming, Object-Oriented Programming, Web Programming and Databases: technical understanding of

implementable requirements; data and interface implications; validation; dependencies; security implications; and the

effect of requirements on later construction.

•  Problem-solving and project foundations: problem decomposition, prioritisation, teamwork, planning, constraints and

reasoned solution development.

SEN381 progression

At NQF Level 8, the progression is from producing isolated analysis or implementation artefacts to engineering a controlled,

traceable and defensible baseline. Requirements, risks, decisions, repository history and evidence must connect to later

architecture, construction, verification, deployment, operation and controlled change. Students must therefore explain not

only what an artefact contains, but why it exists, what evidence supports it, what trade-offs it creates and what downstream

consequences may follow.

Acronym use: Acronyms and abbreviations used in this milestone follow the Acronyms and Abbreviations table in the

2. Milestone Objective

Analyse  the  CivicConnect  problem  within  its  stakeholder,  scope,  schedule,  cost/resource,  quality,  security  and  risk

constraints;  establish  clear,  testable  and  traceable  requirements;  and  create  a  controlled  collaborative  engineering  baseline

that anticipates later architecture, construction, verification, deployment, operation and change.

Central engineering question

What exactly are we committing to engineer, for whom, within what constraints, and what must we consider now to avoid

unnecessarily constraining the project later?

3.1 Progressive GitHub Evidence — Start from Milestone 1

GitHub evidence must be created progressively throughout the milestone. Students must not wait until presentation
preparation to discover that repository history, issues, branches, Pull Requests, reviews, approvals and controlled artefact
changes form part of the assessment evidence.

SEN381 SOFTWARE ENGINEERING 381  |  CIVICCONNECT PROJECT

•  Place appropriate controlled project artefacts in the team repository from the beginning of M1 and update them regularly.
•  Use meaningful issues/tasks, commits, branches and Pull Requests for substantive changes to project artefacts, not only

for application code.

•  Apply the protected-main and required review/approval controls defined in the Master Project Brief from the start of

controlled project work.

•  Repository history must show authentic progression over time. Bulk uploads or reconstructed activity immediately

before assessment do not demonstrate a controlled engineering process.

•  Documentation, requirements, registers, decisions and other engineering artefacts can provide valid repository evidence

before substantial application coding begins.

Key reminder: GitHub is an engineering control and evidence environment, not merely the place where final code is
uploaded.

4. Forward Engineering Considerations

Assignment 1 introduced the idea that some decisions must be considered before they are implemented. M1 now applies that

reasoning to CivicConnect. The team must identify 5–7 project-specific later concerns that already influence current

requirements, constraints, risks or assumptions.

•  Examples may include security, testability, automated build/integration, deployment environment, data

migration/recovery, observability, scalability, maintainability or operational cost.

•  Do not copy Assignment 1 research into M1 as a substitute for project-specific engineering evidence.

•

For each concern, state why it matters now, what later decision/activity it may influence, what information is still

missing, and the risk of ignoring it.

•

It is acceptable—and often correct—to deliberately defer a decision when the team can explain what evidence is still

required before deciding.

8. Indicative Defence Questions

Questions will be selected according to the team's actual evidence. These examples indicate the required depth and are not a

script.

2.

9.1 Research Evidence, Project Evidence and Referencing

Where students rely on external sources, standards, professional guidance or research to support an engineering claim, the
source must be cited appropriately. Research evidence and project evidence are not interchangeable.

•  A citation may support why a practice, standard, quality attribute or engineering concern is relevant.
•  A citation does not prove that the team implemented a control correctly; implementation claims require authentic project

evidence such as repository history, tests, configuration, traceability or other controlled artefacts.

•  Artificial Intelligence output is not a source and does not replace credible referencing or human verification.
